Bias audit

QuotaOS holds no race, ethnicity, or sex data, and the scoring path could not read it if it did. That is deliberate. A model that cannot see a protected characteristic cannot score on it — a materially stronger position than collecting it and promising not to look.

NYC Local Law 144 requires an annual independent audit of selection rates across those categories, so the auditor needs demographics from somewhere. The standard pattern: your organization collects voluntary EEO self-identification through a channel the hiring decision never touches, and the auditor joins it to this export on candidate_key. Wiring demographics into this database would destroy the property that makes the scoring defensible.

The export carries one row per application: the requisition, the resume match score, the AI interview score and recommendation, the engine and exact model id that produced it, and the final stage reached.

Subject rights

Access — GDPR Art. 15

Everything held about one person, as JSON: their record, every application, the full stage history, and every AI interview including the questions, their answers, the per-question scores, and the rationale. Somebody asking what a system decided about them is entitled to the reasoning, not just the verdict. Available from any candidate's row in the candidate pool.

Erasure — GDPR Art. 17

Destroys the candidate and all their application data. Redacted audit entries remain, so you can still show that a decision was made and what it scored, without retaining who it was about. Owner-only and irreversible.

Still on you

  • Commissioning the annual independent bias audit, and publishing its summary — LL144 requires both.
  • Notifying candidates that an automated tool is used, at least 10 business days before use.
  • Deciding which jurisdictions you operate in — Illinois, Colorado, and the EU each add their own requirements.
  • A DPA with Anthropic if candidate data reaches the model, and telling candidates a subprocessor is involved.
  • Having a lawyer read all of this. This page is engineering, not advice.
