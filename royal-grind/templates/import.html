Import a prospect list

A CSV from ZoomInfo, Apollo, Salesforce or HubSpot — or your own spreadsheet. The header row is read to work out which platform it came from, so you are not hand-mapping twenty columns. Nothing is written until you have seen the preview.

Only used as the fallback. A row with an owner column goes to that rep; otherwise the rep whose territory covers the state wins.

Off by default, and that is the recommendation. A vendor marking an address “Verified” is their claim, on their date, by a method you cannot inspect. Left off, it lands as probable with their exact wording preserved, and your first connect promotes it to verified for real. Turned on, their badge becomes yours — and a rep can no longer tell the difference between a number you dialed on Tuesday and one a list broker asserted.

Employees and revenue accept the shapes these exports really contain — $74M, 74,000,000, 201-500 (midpoint), 1,000+, and ZoomInfo's “in 000s”. A cell it cannot read stays empty rather than becoming zero: zero is a claim, and an unreadable cell is not one we are entitled to make.

Three things it does that a loader doesn't

It checks the suppression list first
An import is the likeliest way a number somebody asked you never to call again gets back in. Matching rows still import — the company is still real — but the number is written invalid with the reason, so it can never reach a call list.
It won't overwrite what a rep learned
Re-importing fills blanks only. A rep who wrote “38 loads a month” after asking the customer holds better data than a vendor's estimate, and a second import must not quietly undo that.
Re-importing is the point, not a hazard
Companies are keyed on the source's own record id, else domain, else name + state; contacts on email, else name within the company. The same file applied twice updates in place.
