How the weight is computed, and why it moves slowly. A signal's value per attempt is measured against the org-wide average, then shrunk toward 1.00 in proportion to sample size — at {{ shrink }} attempts the evidence and the prior get equal say. It is then clamped to the range 0.55–1.60. The clamp matters: the sample was drawn by a scoring function that already favored high-scoring signals, so an uncapped loop would score a lucky signal up, call it more, collect more outcomes for it, and score it up again. That is a feedback loop, not learning.
